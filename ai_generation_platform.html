1950 – 1969
Dawn of Symbolic AI
Logic TheoristTuring Test LISPSearch Algorithms Perceptron

The birth of AI as a formal discipline. Researchers believed that human intelligence could be precisely described and simulated with symbolic rules. Computers began solving geometry problems, playing chess, and proving theorems — feats once thought uniquely human.

  • 1950 — Alan Turing proposes the Turing Test in "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"
  • 1956 — Dartmouth Conference coins the term "Artificial Intelligence"
  • 1957 — Frank Rosenblatt builds the Perceptron, the first neural network
  • 1958 — John McCarthy creates LISP, the AI programming language
  • 1965 — ELIZA chatbot demonstrates natural language processing
🕹️
1970 – 1989
Expert Systems & AI Winters
Expert SystemsMYCIN PrologKnowledge Base AI Winter

AI oscillated between hype and disillusionment. Expert systems encoded human domain knowledge into rule-based engines and achieved real commercial success in medicine and finance. But brittle reasoning and massive hardware costs triggered two "AI Winters" — periods of severe funding cuts.

  • 1972 — MYCIN, expert system for medical diagnosis, is developed at Stanford
  • 1973 — Lighthill Report triggers first AI Winter in the UK
  • 1974–80 — First AI Winter: funding dried up globally
  • 1980 — Expert systems boom; XCON saves DEC $40M/year
  • 1987–93 — Second AI Winter follows collapse of LISP machine market
🧠
1990 – 2009
Machine Learning Rise
SVMsDecision Trees Deep BlueBackpropagation Statistical NLP

AI shifted from hand-coded rules to learning from data. Statistical methods, Support Vector Machines, and early neural networks outperformed symbolic approaches. IBM's Deep Blue defeating Kasparov in 1997 captured the world's imagination. The web began generating vast datasets that would fuel future breakthroughs.

  • 1997 — IBM Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov
  • 1998 — LeCun's Convolutional Neural Network reads handwritten digits (MNIST)
  • 2001 — Random Forests algorithm published by Breiman
  • 2006 — Hinton's Deep Belief Networks re-ignite neural network research
  • 2009 — ImageNet large-scale visual database launched
🔬
2010 – 2016
Deep Learning Revolution
CNNsAlexNet GANsResNet GPU ComputingRNNs

GPU-accelerated deep learning demolished benchmarks across vision, speech, and games. AlexNet's 2012 ImageNet victory sparked a gold rush in deep learning. GANs enabled machines to generate photorealistic images. AlphaGo mastered the ancient game of Go — once thought impossible for AI — using deep reinforcement learning.

  • 2012 — AlexNet wins ImageNet, slashing error rates by 41%; Deep Learning era begins
  • 2014 — Ian Goodfellow invents Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)
  • 2015 — ResNet (152-layer) surpasses human accuracy on ImageNet
  • 2016 — AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol 4–1 at the game of Go
  • 2016 — TensorFlow 1.0 released; democratizes deep learning
⚡
2017 – 2022
Transformer Age
TransformersBERT GPT-2/3Attention DALL·EScaling Laws

The paper "Attention Is All You Need" introduced the Transformer architecture, reshaping every AI domain. BERT and GPT-3 demonstrated that language models pre-trained at scale could adapt to almost any task. DALL·E brought text-to-image generation. Scaling laws revealed a simple truth: bigger models + more data = better intelligence.

  • 2017 — "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the Transformer (Vaswani et al.)
  • 2018 — BERT from Google sets new records across 11 NLP benchmarks
  • 2019 — GPT-2 deemed "too dangerous to release"; later fully released
  • 2020 — GPT-3 (175B params) shocks the world; few-shot learning emerges
  • 2021 — DALL·E and CLIP: AI learns to link language and images
  • 2022 — Stable Diffusion, Midjourney; image generation goes mainstream
🚀
2023 – PRESENT
Generative AI Explosion
ChatGPTLLMs RLHFMultimodal AI AgentsClaudeGemini

ChatGPT reached 100 million users in 2 months — the fastest product adoption in history. Large Language Models became creative co-pilots, coders, and reasoning engines. Multimodal AI unified vision, audio, and language. AI Agents began autonomously browsing the web, writing code, and orchestrating complex workflows.

  • 2023 — ChatGPT reaches 100M users in 2 months; GPT-4 released
  • 2023 — Claude, Gemini, Llama usher in a competitive LLM era
  • 2023 — Stable Diffusion XL, Midjourney v5 push image quality boundaries
  • 2024 — Sora generates cinematic video from text prompts
  • 2024 — AI coding agents (Cursor, Devin) reshape software engineering
  • 2025 — Multimodal reasoning models become industry standard
